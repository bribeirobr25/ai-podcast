## 2. Executive finding

The repository accurately captures the **October 26 editorial concept and historical Node.js/Anam architecture**, but it was not kept current after the newer **zero-code / native voice app** exploration. It also compresses different types of validation into vague statements. The project is **conceptually developed; prototype assets exist; a reliable live production workflow has not been independently demonstrated**. The correct next milestone is evidence from a real conversation and recording, not more platform infrastructure.

The project's product is the **quality of recurring, distinct AI conversations**. Zoom, Google Meet, standalone phones, avatars and the custom app are replaceable transport/presentation choices. That distinction should govern all development.

## 5. Decisions, options and open questions

### Confirmed direction (D)
- Name: **BAR Podcast**, hosted/facilitated by **BAR**.
- AI models are **guests rather than obedient assistants**. They may propose identity, appearance, voice, boundaries and recording instructions through invitation and pre-interview; the human producer retains responsibility.
- Guests can ask, disagree, pass, revise, refuse and sometimes remain silent within platform limits.
- Preserve traceability of guest outputs versus producer-selected prompts, tooling, editing and constraints.
- Working support for **Brazilian Portuguese and English** is a requirement for a future full workflow.
- Evaluate whether the **same guest is worth hearing again**, including memory and relationship continuity.

### Active experiment, not a proven product decision (P)
- Try laptop (BAR) + phone/tablet (native AI voice applications), potentially with two AI guests speaking in the same physical room. Test acoustics, turn-taking and recording.
- Try Zoom/Google Meet with one AI guest, controlled observer and explicit *both-way* audio test; test operating-system limitations before selecting a meeting platform.
- Optionally give recurring guests human-managed accounts or mailboxes. No accounts, autonomous permissions or OAuth delegation have been demonstrated.
- A software meeting participant / audio bridge can be considered **only after** testing zero-code routing.

### Deferred (P)
- Multiple simultaneous avatars, AI-to-AI salon between episodes, 24/7 room, shared long-term memory service, autonomous outbound email/action agents, robot experiments, a new custom recording platform.

### Open decisions (U)
- Which native voice application(s), guest model(s), hardware/OS versions and recorder will be used in the first experiment?
- What is the primary language and editorial topic of the first publishable pilot?
- How will a returning guest's identity and memory be kept consistent if the native consumer app uses a different model/memory policy than the historical API version?
- What permissions, notices and rights apply to recording/reusing provider voices or avatars and circulating episodes?
- What happens if the same-device meeting routing cannot carry both incoming and outgoing audio?

## 6. Reconciled product architecture

**Layer 1 — identity and editorial contract:** guest invitation, chosen preferences, boundaries, model/provider identity, history, interventions and memory evidence. This is conceptually independent of the audiovisual transport, but not automatically portable between providers.

**Layer 2 — conversation engine:** a consumer voice app (quick experiment) **or** the original API-based model orchestration (later, for precise control). These are distinct stacks; a commercial app does not guarantee API model equivalence.

**Layer 3 — transport and recording:** co-located devices + laptop recorder (Experiment 0), meeting application + verified audio loop (Experiment 1), or custom Node.js/Anam studio (historical Option C). Select only what the current test requires.

**Layer 4 — production and evidence:** recordings, transcripts, who/what supplied each instruction, consent and vendor permissions, editorial review, independent listener feedback. Maintain a manual experiment log until tooling proves necessary.

## 7. Recommended test gates

1. **G0: conversation only.** Laptop records, one native AI voice application on a second device. Test hearing, intelligible replies, interruptions and short unedited conversation quality.
2. **G1: optional two-AI room.** Add another voice app on a third device; measure reciprocal hearing and turn-taking. Do not equate merely having both apps running with actual two-way conversation.
3. **G2: meeting routing.** Test a single AI guest through Zoom/Meet; independently check the remote observer's hearing of the AI and the AI's hearing of the meeting, as well as echo and background-session stability. Fail means switch to separate devices or virtual routing, not rewrite the whole platform.
4. **G3: returning guest.** A second recorded interaction tests what the AI demonstrably recalls, what was supplied by the human, and what changed. Do not label native app memory "AI-authored canonical memory" without evidence.
5. **G4: edited pilot.** Select the strongest interaction, verify permissions, label the AI participation clearly, publish only after a human content/legal review.

**Gate evidence:** record date, device OS versions, meeting/voice app versions, model label as surfaced by app, language, exact audio path, raw clip, failure modes, human interventions, and explicit pass/fail. An unedited extract and transcript matter more than a simulated demo.
