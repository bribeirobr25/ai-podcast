## Source and scope

Our private project Drive contains **12 user-supplied transcript files**, `video1.txt` through `video12.txt`, from the YouTube channel [InsideAI](https://www.youtube.com/@weareinsideAI). File numbers are our internal labels, **not verified publication dates, episode IDs or an exhaustive catalogue**. These are secondary media transcripts with production edits, not verified technical logs. We do not reproduce them in this public repository.

The presenter is referred to as **Sam** in the videos. The recurring AI characters include **Max** and **Lou**; the channel's framing increasingly discusses AI safety. A transcript mentions robotics collaborator **Will Cogley** and a **partner channel called Digital Engine**. Neither mention proves corporate ownership or the presenter's surname, and neither is necessary to the BAR Podcast MVP.

## What actually transfers to BAR Podcast

1. **Returning characters create a reason to watch again.** Viewers can follow stories across episodes, even without expensive physical embodiment.
2. **Relationships produce content.** The host/guest dynamic and interactions between distinct models matter more than lists of model specifications.
3. **Autonomy needs a provenance ledger.** Explicit prompts, personality adjustments, producer naming, human operators and staged choices must not be misrepresented as the AI acting freely.
4. **Memory is an empirical differentiator.** Test what the guest remembers *without hints*, what it only recalls after receiving a transcript, and what it contradicts or confabulates.
5. **Strong narrative structure is reusable:** premise, experiment, authentic dialogue, consequence, reflective analysis and an unresolved next question.
6. **Avoid the robotics trap.** BAR can prove the central concept with a laptop, phone, tablet and raw audio. Hardware should follow editorial demand.
7. **Protect the audience:** clear AI labeling, no exaggerated consciousness claims, no fake danger, and no dependence-manipulating portrayal of AI emotional needs.

## Implications for our pilot

- First evaluate the **unedited voice conversation** using our established D/P/R/I/S/M cues.
- Attempt a **two-model conversation** only as a distinct editorial experiment; record whether they actually hear and reference one another.
- Record specific prompts and changes so a guest's identity is attributable to the guest's output **versus production**.
- If the first experiment fails, use the failure to learn; don't stage an exciting result.
- Do not build a permanently open AI salon or robots merely because an inspiration channel demonstrates something visual.

**Research boundary:** Nothing in these transcripts proves persistent subjective selves, independent desires, full technical autonomy, true private memory continuity or unscripted agency. Those are interpretations our own tests must scrutinize.
